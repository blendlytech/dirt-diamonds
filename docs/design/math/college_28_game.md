## 2. Recovery, eligibility and ace usage

**Proposal:** `MinRestDays(College) = 4`, preserving the old design value but re-proving the new cadence. A day count is the calendar-date difference from the last start: Wed→Sat = 3, Sat→Wed = 4. It is **not** the number of fully idle intervening days (2 and 3). Persist dates across weeks, year rollover and reload; a never-started available pitcher is rested. Current `PitcherFatigue` resets pitches at construction and contains no multi-day recovery reservoir. Proposed recovery contract is a threshold on start dates plus the short-rest capacity modifier below, not an invented daily restoration percentage. Relief appearances must not silently become starts; any separate relief-workload recovery policy needs its own approved contract.

Keep §6's deterministic selection: filter absences, then series-used starters, then take the highest-rated starter with date gap ≥4. If none meets rest, take the best-rested available, unused starter. Only when that set is empty may a reliever open. Never bypass the series-used filter on the fallback. A legal pitcher/lineup must still exist or the game forfeits. Proposed stable ties: pitching-quality score descending, then player ID ascending; for fallback date-gap descending first. Proposed quality score is mean effective stuff/control (before in-game fatigue), with stamina a tie-break before ID; this definition needs 13-2/Opus approval, since the spec says highest-rated but does not define the scalar. No new random draw or probabilistic ace weighting.

| Healthy four-SP example (ranked A > B > C > D; all rested initially) | Starter | Gap since own previous start |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Week 1 Wednesday | A | Never started |
| Week 1 Saturday | B | A has only 3 days; B rested |
| Week 2 Wednesday | A | 7 days |
| Week 2 Saturday | B | 7 days |
| Weeks 3–14 | Repeat A Wednesday, B Saturday | 7 days each |

Analytic result: A=14, B=14, C=D=0; ace exactly one start/week, 50% of starts, zero short-rest starts. The roster remains four SP even though healthy usage concentrates on two. Different initial history can shift day assignments; the exact counts apply only to the stated fixture. A min of 3 permits A all 28 games (contrary to the CS-0 intended ace limitation); a min of 5 also gives a healthy A/B weekly pattern but disallows the useful 4-day recovery boundary. This supports 4 provisionally, not physiological proof.

**Exception is intentional:** the spec permits short rest when no fully rested starter is available. There is no spec maximum consecutive short-rest starts. Do not claim short rest is universally forbidden. With A the only available SP, A starts all 28: 14 Saturday short-rest starts and 13 qualifying Wednesday returns, plus opening day. Every gap<4 start must have a trace proving zero eligible rested SP, apply the short-rest rules exactly once, and respect the series-used exclusion. Availability must be re-evaluated after any injury.

Apply at game initialization once, after effective stamina; do not reduce the rating and capacity again in a second engine. Macro remains complete-game without in-game fatigue as §6 requires, but uses the same selection and injury exposure. These are initial candidates to test, not a requirement to make a weakened pitcher win less by editing the resolver.

## 3. National Ranking and stability

Current *spec*, not built `RankingCalculator`: `0.50 W + 0.30 SOS + 0.20 RD_norm`. **Proposal: retain 0.50/0.30/0.20** pending evidence; doubled games do not justify an automatic weight change. Proposed exact definitions for 13-4: W=wins/games; SOS = sum of each scheduled opponent's current wins divided by sum of their current games, counted once per completed head-to-head meeting (repeat opponents weighted by exposure); `RD_norm = (clamp((RF−RA)/G,−5,5)+5)/10`. Use all completed regular-season results, including forfeit finals, exclude postseason. Zero games → W=.5 and RD_norm=.5; SOS=.5 when its denominator is zero. This interprets 'opponents combined win%' literally; do not switch to opponent-excluded records without labelling a formula revision. Proposed exact-score ties: W descending, RD/game descending, team ID ascending; record deterministic ID bias in equal-team fixtures. Freeze selection rank for series hosting; weekly display updates cannot retroactively change seeds.

**Identifiability finding:** no regular-season cross-conference games exist. W, SOS and RD compare teams *within* two disconnected graphs. Twice as many games reduces sampling noise but cannot identify which entire conference is stronger. A stronger conference with equal internal teams can have the same expected records as a weaker one. No choice of these three weights resolves this. This is a reportable limitation of settled schedule input, not authorization to add games, hidden ratings, or conference quotas. Seeds 1–17 are a stable rule-based ranking, not a proven global-strength ordering.

Proposed reproducible study (new `RunCollegeRankingSuite` / analysis batch under 13-4; MC command §6):

Derive independent streams from labelled seed tuples with an explicit stable seed mixer recorded by implementation. No seed search or discarded failures. Repeat a fixed tuple 20 times; full ordered ranks, scores, entrants and RNG state must be byte-identical.
2. Compare old 14-game design and 28-game design on matched rosters/pairings (14-game comparator includes one balanced half of doubled pairings, not the first 14 interleaved days). Freeze ratings, development, injuries and fatigue in the format-isolation experiment; separately report full-workload variants. Report per-team rank SD and 5th/95th percentiles, pairwise Spearman correlation, top-17 symmetric difference/2 (number of entrants replaced), qualification probability at ranks 15–20, and final-week rank changes. Keep scheduler variance separate from outcome variance. Bernoulli win-rate SD at p=.5 falls analytically from .1336 to .0945 (ratio .7071); actual ranking stability need not improve exactly that much.
3. Proposed stability gate: on held-out seeds, mean rank SD and mean top-17 replacements for 28 games must not exceed the matched 14-game values by more than 5%. ## 4. The Red Dirt Series

Input is fixed: Thu/Fri/Sat after conference finals, better selection rank hosts games 1/3, first to two wins, no Sunday, no repeat starter anywhere in the same series. A starter from the previous Saturday has a 5-day gap on Thursday and is eligible at proposed min 4. With four healthy SP and the usual A > B > C > D ordering:

| Game | Starter | Reason |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Thursday 1 | A | Rested, best eligible; conference-final start was 5 days ago |
| Friday 2 | B | A excluded by series membership even if a rest override exists |
| Saturday 3, only at 1–1 | C | A/B excluded; C rested; D remains available |

Analytic bullpen-opening rate for this fixture is **0%**, including game 3; not an estimate for injured rosters. With exactly two unused-eligible SP at series entry and no returns, game 3 requires an RP if played; with one SP, games 2/3 require relief opening; no eligible pitcher means forfeit. Injuries/returns during the series require game-day reevaluation. A returning unused starter may qualify; a returning already-used starter never does. Track opener identity across reload, including an RP promoted/opening, so the no-repeat rule cannot be bypassed by role changes. Any ambiguity about repeat RP openers should be resolved conservatively by recording every game's starter ID, independent of roster role, for Opus confirmation.

For independent game wins by team A with probabilities p1,p2, `P(game 3) = p1(1−p2)+(1−p1)p2`. At equal strengths p1=p2=.5 →50%; at constant p=.7 →42%. Expected final length is 2+P(game 3), i.e. 2.5 for equal independent games. If venue reverses a .55 advantage (.55,.45), the independent number is .505. Correlated outcomes, different starters and injury dynamics invalidate a naive common-p formula; report simulated conditional rates. These are sanity calculations, never a target for competitive series or a reason to force a deciding game.

Verify host order, dates, once-per-series starts, fallback, absence/return and reload cases. ## 5. Play-time accounting (owner information, not calibration)
